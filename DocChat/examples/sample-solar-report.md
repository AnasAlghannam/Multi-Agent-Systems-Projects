## Panel Types

### Monocrystalline Panels

Monocrystalline panels are cut from a single silicon crystal. They reach
efficiencies of 20% to 24%, the highest of the mainstream residential options,
and perform comparatively well in low light. They carry a typical product
warranty of 25 years and cost roughly 15% more per watt than polycrystalline
alternatives.

### Polycrystalline Panels

Polycrystalline panels are made from multiple silicon fragments melted together.
Efficiency ranges from 15% to 17%. They are cheaper to manufacture but need more
roof area for the same output, and their typical warranty is 20 years.

### Thin-Film Panels

Thin-film panels reach 10% to 13% efficiency. They are lightweight and flexible,
which suits unusual mounting surfaces, but they degrade faster than crystalline
panels and are usually warranted for 10 to 15 years.

## Inverters

String inverters convert the combined DC output of a panel array to AC. They are
the cheapest option, but shading on any single panel reduces output across the
whole string.

Microinverters are installed per panel, so shading affects only the shaded panel.
They cost about 20% more for a typical installation and are usually warranted for
25 years, against 10 to 12 years for string inverters.

## Performance Factors

Output falls by roughly 0.4% per degree Celsius above 25 degrees, so ventilation
behind the panels matters in hot climates. Panels degrade about 0.5% per year,
meaning a 25-year-old array typically still produces around 87% of its original
rated output.

Optimal orientation in the northern hemisphere is true south at a tilt equal to
the site latitude. A 15-degree deviation from optimal costs about 3% of annual
production.

## Maintenance

Cleaning twice a year recovers 3% to 5% of output in dusty regions. Annual
inspection of wiring and mounting hardware is recommended. Inverters are the most
common failure point and are typically replaced once during the array's lifetime.
